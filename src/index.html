Bowling Speed Gun

Film a delivery, mark two frames, get the speed.

1 Pitch setup

Backyard or indoor pitch? Pace out the distance from where the ball leaves the hand to where you'll mark it arriving and type it in.

2 Get a video

On a phone, Record opens your camera app. Use Slo-mo mode if your phone has it (120 or 240 fps is far more accurate than 30 fps).

More filming tips
  • Film side-on, about level with the middle of the pitch, so you can see the bowler's hand and the batter's end in the same shot.
  • Keep the phone still: lean it on something or use a tripod. Don't zoom or pan.
  • Plenty of light means a sharper ball and less blur.
  • Mark the arrival at the same spot your distance measures to (the stumps or the crease).
  • Umpire view (filming from behind the bowler's stumps) works too: pick "Umpire view" in auto-detect, or mark the frames by hand. It's less precise than side-on, because the ball comes almost straight at the camera, so use slo-mo and keep the batter in shot.
